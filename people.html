Graduate Students

KH

Kyla Hudson

Ph.D. Student · Chemistry

B.S. Chemistry, Mercer University

Kyla came to Stanford from Mercer University, where she graduated as Outstanding Senior in Chemistry and earned a Royal Society of Chemistry Certificate of Undergraduate Excellence. As an Amgen Scholar and WAVE Fellow she worked with Mitchio Okumura at Caltech, using cavity ring-down spectroscopy to probe the reaction mechanisms behind aromatic, aerosol, and soot formation — chemistry relevant all the way out to the atmosphere of Titan. She has also measured atmospheric mercury in Guyana through Mercer On Mission.

AA

Ace Aguilar

Ph.D. Student · Chemistry

B.S. Chemical Biology, UC Berkeley (2022)

Ace is a Stanford EDGE Fellow and a Sarafan ChEM-H Chemistry–Biology Interface Fellow, admitted in 2024. Before graduate school he spent two years at Berkeley Lab’s Advanced Biofuels and Bioproducts Process Development Unit working on biopolymer processing, interned at Amyris on high-throughput screening, and served as a ChEM-H research associate in the Khosla lab studying polyketide biosynthesis as a platform for drug discovery. He holds the George C. Pimentel Award in Chemical Education, which recognizes first-generation college students pursuing graduate study in chemistry, and works to make academia more equitable for students from underrepresented backgrounds.

BD

Bailey Daymo

Ph.D. Student · Chemical Engineering

B.S. Chemical Engineering, UC Berkeley (2025)

Bailey joined Stanford in 2025 from UC Berkeley, after working on the biosynthesis of antibiotic natural products from oral bacteria — purifying polyketide synthase and nonribosomal peptide synthetase assembly-line proteins and characterizing them by HPLC and mass spectrometry. That work became a 2024 Biochemistry paper reconstituting the salivabactin pathway in vitro, pinning down the minimal enzyme set, and uncovering an unusual thioesterase domain that forms a terminal olefin.

How we work

Everyone here owns
a question.

Own the question

Students drive and shape their own projects. The group has its first experiments mapped out; from there we follow where the science leads.

Cross the aisle

Chemistry, chemical engineering, microbiology, and analytics in one room. The most useful ideas tend to arrive from the discipline next door.

Leave it better

Curiosity, rigor, and generosity toward colleagues are the prerequisites we treat as non-negotiable — for everyone, from every background.
